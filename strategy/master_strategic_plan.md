## 1. Situation summary

**The company.** Durent Support (founded 2025, Jabodetabek, founder/CEO Marco "Coco" Saputra Tedy, bootstrapped with Skystar Ventures backing) is a full-service production/event support vendor: equipment rental, crew catering, locations, transport, and crew support. ~15 months of operation: ~Rp 191jt total revenue, 56% gross margin, ROI 5.4x, average job Rp 1.7jt, 29% repeat rate. Q1 2026: Rp 55.4jt gross from 11 clients — all from warm referral.

**The pivot.** Durent is restructuring into a holding with two segments. Film/commercial ("Location Department as a Service") is stalled on sales; the event segment (bazaar/campus/expo organizers) is the growth priority because a single bazaar can net Rp 50jt+ versus thin film day-rates. Production houses are explicitly avoided (they have direct vendor relationships; prior cold outreach produced zero leads).

**The constraint.** Durent needs **≥ Rp 100jt net cash by end of 2026** — Rp 50jt for a forced office move in December, Rp 50jt runway. That gives roughly **four months (Sep–Dec)** to generate it. Every recommendation below is sequenced against that clock.

**The diagnosis — refined by the research.** The owner's read is that the offering works (two large deals, Rp 50jt+ and Rp 100jt+, nearly closed and failed for reasons unrelated to price or execution) and the problem is volume/follow-through. The research sharpens this in three ways:

1. **It is a ticket-size problem at least as much as a volume problem.** Durent's Rp 1.7jt average job is *below the minimum order at which tent specialists will even engage* (Rp 2.5jt in-city [snippet]). A real mid-size bazaar tent job bills ~Rp 18jt (Expo UMKM Jaksel benchmark: 20× sarnafil 4×4, 2 days [snippet]). Three Rp 18jt-class jobs beat thirty Rp 1.7jt jobs on revenue, margin, and effort — and the existing pipeline (50-tenda/13-day tender, PT Furindo Artha Mas) already points at that class.
2. **The stated differentiation will not survive contact with the market.** "One-stop event solution" is the standard marketing copy of at least seven established Jabodetabek incumbents (Eventara 10+ yrs, Raja Alat Event "One Stop Solution Event Industry" since 2010, Akusewa, Over Organizer, Sakata, Seventent, Zein 15+ yrs). The claimed moat list (PT, vendor agreements, embedded-supplier status, pay-on-time) is a *supply-side* list pitched to the *demand* side: PT is a tender gate every serious rival already has; vendor agreements buy reliability, not pricing power; "embedded supplier" is what the incumbents already are and Durent is not yet.
3. **What actually is defensible: cross-category coordination.** No incumbent found bundles what Durent bundles. Equipment houses do not sell HT, catering, manpower, or transport — those are three separate industries that don't cross-sell. An organizer needing tenda + HT + crew today runs three procurement threads; that friction is Durent's real product, and it is the one claim in Durent's pitch that survives competitive scrutiny.

**The market backdrop (trend research, all cited in `research/market_trends.md`).** The event market is split: government-driven MICE is collapsing under Inpres 01/2025 budget efficiency (players report 60–65% declines Q1 2026; central-to-region transfers cut ~25% in 2026), while private/consumer events are booming (Jakarta Kreatif Festival 2026 transacted Rp 55B vs Rp 21B in 2025; exhibition industry projected +15% overall). A continuous Sep–Dec Jabodetabek demand corridor runs through Durent's deadline: JIExpo trade-expo cluster (2–12 Sep) → Pestapora 25–27 Sep (Boss Creator is a named warm target) → Comic Con PIK2 (3–4 Oct) → Comifuro ICE BSD (31 Oct–1 Nov) → INTEX/Homelife (25–28 Nov) → the year-end bazaar wave (late Nov–early Jan). Campus PKKMB 2026 is finishing now; near-term campus revenue is the Sep–Dec festival season, and PKKMB outreach targets the 2027 cycle. The film line is soft (H1 2026 admissions −16.7%) with real, decade-old competitors (Fixer Indonesia, Mbrella, Wind Up, Celluloid Pact) — the pivot is competitively justified, not just financially.

**Most time-critical fact:** APKASI Otonomi Expo 2026 runs **27–29 August at ICE BSD Hall 3–3A — five days from today** — and Durent holds six named warm contacts there.

## 2. Strategic options considered

### Option A — Volume play: scale cold outreach on the current offer and ticket size
Push the validated cold-email motion (100+ emails → ~12 replies) much harder at the current Rp 1.7jt average job.
**Rejected as the primary strategy.** At Rp 1.7jt average and 56% GPM, reaching Rp 100jt net requires on the order of a hundred jobs in four months — operationally implausible, and it keeps Durent transacting below the specialists' minimum-order floor, in the most price-discoverable tier of the market (Over Organizer publishes Rp 25–60k/m² tenda pricing; marketplaces make the commodity floor visible). Outreach stays — but as a channel serving bigger deals, not as the strategy.

### Option B — Asset play: buy inventory (tenda fleet) and compete head-on as an equipment house
**Rejected.** Tents are a logistics business (trucks, storage, wash/repair, install crews) where incumbents have 10–16 years of amortised fleets; Durent would enter as the highest-cost operator, and the capex directly cannibalises the Rp 100jt cash target. Booth fabrication (Layer D) is even more clearly out: it needs owned workshops and rigging compliance — treat booth contractors as partners/referral sources, never rivals.

### Option C — Government/tender play: lean on PT registration and chase pemda/GOR + APKASI-follow-on budgets
**Rejected as the 2026 cash plan; retained as a 2027 lane.** Trend research shows exactly this money is what Inpres 01/2025 gutted (60–65% MICE declines, 25% transfer cuts). Tender money is slow and shrinking this year. PT registration is still the right unlock — but it is a gate, not a moat, and nothing about the Dec 2026 target should depend on it.

### Option D — Coordination play: reposition as the cross-category operations layer, raise ticket size to the Rp 18jt+ class, ride the Sep–Dec private-events corridor ← **RECOMMENDED**
Sell "we run your event's physical operations" — one contract, one invoice, one accountable party across tenda + HT + crew + catering + transport — to private/consumer event organizers who lack vendor networks, at bundle ticket sizes (Rp 15–50jt), through the one channel incumbents don't run (outbound + nurture). Selectively own the HT fleet (small capex, highest utilisation, least price-shopped); broker everything bulky. Details and rationale in §3.

### Option E — Retreat: refocus on film/commercial where the identity is strongest
**Rejected.** Film demand is soft and structurally constrained (screen scarcity, −16.7% H1 admissions), the segment has entrenched internationally-oriented competitors, Durent's own data shows cold outreach there yields zero, and IFFa is best read not as empty white space but as a latent, state-backed entrant (PFN/Telkom, published roadmap covering Durent's exact services — dormant but policy-supported). Film stays as a maintain/warm-referral line; "Location Department as a Service" stays as its label; no new investment.

## 3. Recommended direction: the Coordination Play (Option D)

**Positioning (adopting brand-specialist's recommendation verbatim):**
- Parent: **"Durent Support — coordinated production & event operations in Jabodetabek."**
- Event line: **"Satu Operasi, Bukan Lima Vendor"** — *"We run your event's equipment, comms, crew, catering, and transport as one coordinated operation — not five separate vendors you have to manage yourself."*
- Film line: keep "Location Department as a Service" unchanged, zero new investment.
- Retire "one-stop" and "embedded supplier" as headline claims. "One-stop" is category wallpaper (used near-verbatim by Eventara, Raja Alat Event, Akusewa, Seventent); "embedded supplier" is a status incumbents hold and Durent doesn't yet — keep it as a relationship promise to specific repeat clients, not a market claim.

**Why this wins, grounded in the three research files:**

1. **It is the only uncontested ground.** Competitor research found the coordination bundle structurally unoccupied — layers A (equipment), C (HT/comms), and E (manpower) are separate industries that do not cross-sell. Durent already operates across all of them; the claim is provable today, not aspirational.
2. **It is the only way the margin survives.** Durent's 56% blended GPM implies ~2.3× markup on items whose upstream price is one Google search away (Over Organizer publishes its full price list). Sold as line items ("tenda, and also HT, and also crew"), that markup is arithmetic any buyer can do and will be competed away. Sold as "we run your event's physical operations," the billable unit is coordination + advisory + single-point accountability — which buyer-behaviour research shows organizers actually select on ("vendor profesional biasanya akan membantu Anda menghitung…" — advisory is a stated purchase criterion).
3. **It matches the resilient side of a split market.** Private/consumer bazaars, brand expos, pop-culture cons, campus fests, and corporate year-end events are growing while government MICE contracts. The ICP ("organizers without their own vendor network") lives almost entirely on the private side.
4. **It solves the stated internal problems, not just the market problem.** Vendor fatigue is fixed by the rate card *plus* fewer, bigger, better-qualified quotes; the near-miss deals ("panitia menghilang", policy changes) are fixed by the nurture cadence and institution-level relationships (§4, W2/W4); the "no consistent way to get sales" problem is fixed by naming outbound+nurture as the primary channel — the one channel every SEO-dependent incumbent ignores, and the one Durent has already validated (100+ emails → ~12 replies). It needs zero capital.
5. **The economics reach the target.** The Rp 100jt goal at ~56% GPM needs roughly Rp 180–200jt of gross billing in four months. In the Rp 15–50jt ticket class, that is **5–10 closed bundles** — against a warm pipeline that already contains a 50-tenda/13-day tender, PT Furindo Artha Mas (recurring), six named APKASI contacts, Pestapora/Boss Creator, and a Sep–Dec calendar full of qualified targets. That is a plausible number of closes; a hundred Rp 1.7jt jobs is not.

**Asset posture (from competitor-analyst §7.2, adopted):** split by asset class — **own HT, broker tents, never own booths.** HT is needed at essentially every event, has the highest utilisation, the least price-shopping, trivial logistics (fits in a car), and converts Durent from reseller to vendor-of-record on the one item every organizer needs — a legitimate reason to be in the room for everything else. **Condition:** fleet capex must be sized against the Rp 100jt cash target and ideally funded from Sep–Oct closed-deal cashflow, not from reserves. Tenda ownership is explicitly rejected (see Option B).

**Pricing posture:** publish the rate card **for bundles, not units**, quoted **all-in** (transport + install + dismantle + tax) — all-in is what buyers are explicitly coached to demand, and bundle pricing legitimately avoids exposing per-unit markup against published upstream prices. Hold pricing; do not discount — EO package prices are up 10–15% YoY, and vendor-side capacity is loosening (MICE layoffs), which means Durent's *costs* can fall while prices hold.

## 4. Roadmap

11. **2027 setup:** complete PT registration and enter the tender/LPSE lane (sold as capability, never as advantage); pursue official-vendor appointment at the recurring expos identified via the APKASI question; deepen campus institutional agreements; revisit IFFa as a potential *partner* if it revives.

### What to fix first (priority order if capacity is short)

## 5. Risks and open questions for the owner (do not let these be assumed)

1. **Owned vs brokered inventory (tenda and HT) — the single most important unresolved fact.** Everything about margin defensibility turns on it. The research *inferred* brokered from vendor-fatigue and the moat list; Coco must confirm.
2. **Decompose the 56% GPM by service line.** Equipment-only margin decides whether the brokered model survives contact with published competitor pricing.
3. **Capital available for the HT fleet before December**, given the Rp 100jt target.
4. **Domain question:** which of durentsupport.com / durent-support.my.id is canonical, and who controls the other?
5. **Venue vendor-list policy at JIExpo/ICE BSD** — no published daftar rekanan found [UNVERIFIED]; a short phone call settles it, and the APKASI question does the same at organizer level.
6. **Bypass risk on brokered jobs:** an organizer who sees the subvendor's brand on delivered tents can call them next year. Mitigations: neutral/Durent-branded touchpoints where feasible, Durent-owned HT as the vendor-of-record anchor, and contracts at the coordination layer. The film-side "anti-bypass architecture" has no event-side equivalent yet — build one.
7. **Event business model is explicitly "belum fix"** (context §4): this plan resolves it in the coordination direction; Coco should consciously ratify that rather than inherit it.
8. **Purchasing-power softness** (middle class shrinking, defensive consumption): mitigated by targeting value-oriented bazaar formats, which are outperforming — but expect price sensitivity in the SME-tenant-funded segment.
