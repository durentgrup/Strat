## 1. What the existing brand assets actually say

### 1.1 The name and mark
"DuRent" = **durian + rent** — a local wordplay pun. The logo renders the "D" as a spiky durian silhouette (visible husk texture on the ascender), set in a bold, rounded, geometric sans-serif, dark near-black on transparent, with "Support" in a smaller matching weight underneath. It's playful, distinctly Indonesian/local-market (durian is a culturally loaded, affectionate reference — not neutral like a generic Latin-root name), and confident without being cute.

Critically: **the pun is about the company's function (rental) and locale (durian = Southeast Asian), not about film or locations specifically.** Nothing in the mark itself is film-industry-coded.

### 1.2 The stated positioning (per `durent-context.md` §3)
> "Location Department as a Service" — website live, 20+ locations onboarded, "AI Scout" for location scouting.

This is industry-insider language. "Location department" is a specific film/TV production-department term (the crew function that scouts, secures, and manages shoot locations). It's a sharp, credible line **to a producer or production manager** — it signals "we are the outsourced version of a function you already know you need." It communicates nothing to someone who has never worked on a film set.

### 1.3 What the company's actual event sales material says (Proposal Event support.pdf)
This is the more revealing artifact. It's a live, in-market proposal deck used with an actual event prospect (a university wisuda/graduation ceremony). Its self-description, verbatim:

> "Kami merupakan vendor yang menyediakan keperluan event seperti: Penyewaan barang-barang event (Tenda, Meja, Kipas, dsb.), Peralatan komunikasi (HT dan Solidcom), Penyediaan Konsumsi (Nasi box dan Prasmanan). Semua layanan terintegrasi menjadi satu di ekosistem durent support."
> ("We are a vendor providing event necessities... All services are integrated into one ecosystem, durent support.")

The phrase **"Location Department as a Service" does not appear anywhere in this document.** When Durent actually had to sell to an event buyer, the team didn't stretch the film positioning to fit — they dropped it and wrote plain, generic "vendor" copy instead. That's not a hypothetical brand-fit problem; it's evidence the fit problem is already live and the team has already, informally, worked around it. The workaround (generic "vendor") is functional but flat — it doesn't carry any of the differentiation the company actually has (one-stop integration, embedded-supplier reliability, moat-in-progress per context §9). It reads like every other tenda/sound rental vendor an organizer already has fatigue with.

### 1.4 Visual system (from the proposal deck — the only in-market event-facing visual asset available)
- Dark, near-black/navy backdrops; moody stadium-floodlight and city-skyline photography, desaturated.
- Bold, rounded, high-weight sans-serif headlines (same type family as the wordmark) in white.
- A restrained gold/champagne accent line — the one "premium" signal in an otherwise monochrome palette.
- Glassmorphism pill-shaped cards for package/price tables — clean, modern, price-forward.
- Product-cutout photography (tents, chairs, AC units, sound equipment) over the mood backgrounds.

This visual system is **not film-specific.** Stadium floodlights and dark cinematic staging read equally well as "concert/event production" or "film set" — it's an atmosphere of scale and professionalism, not a location-scouting visual. **This is good news: the visual system does not need to be rebuilt or split by segment.** It already generalizes.

## 2. Brand coherence assessment: does "Location Department as a Service" work for both segments?

**No — and the company's own sales material already proves it doesn't.**

| | Film/Commercial | Event |
|---|---|---|
| Positioning fit | Strong — "location department" is exact insider vocabulary for this ICP | Meaningless — bazaar/campus/expo organizers don't have or think in terms of a "location department" |
| What ICP actually wants (context §5) | Locations, crew, equipment, catering as a production support function | Tenda, HT, equipment rental from a full-service vendor — commodity-adjacent, price- and reliability-driven |
| Evidence of mismatch | — | Company's own proposal deck abandoned the tagline entirely in favor of generic "vendor" language |
| Strategic weight now (context §4) | Deprioritized — sales stalled here | **Priority** — the segment being actively grown |

The mark and visual system flex fine across both. **The positioning line does not.** Forcing "Location Department as a Service" onto the event segment would be actively counterproductive: it's off-vocabulary for the buyer, undersells the tenda/HT/equipment offer that's actually converting, and doesn't address the one real objection this ICP has (vendor fatigue, context §8) — a "location department" doesn't sound like the cure for having too many disposable vendors.

## 3. Recommendations

### 3.1 Brand architecture: one parent, two segment lines

Keep **Durent Support** as the single parent/holding brand — the name, mark, and visual system are sound and shouldn't change. Under it, run two distinct positioning lines, not one stretched to cover both:

- **Parent-level frame (holding, investor-facing, footer/about-us level):**
  **"Durent Support — the embedded supplier for productions and events in Jabodetabek."**
  This uses "embedded supplier," which is already the company's own chosen differentiator (context §9: embedded supplier status vs. one-off vendor) — it's honest about the model (managed/full-service, not marketplace) and it's the one phrase that genuinely describes both segments without contradiction. This is the throughline that makes "one company, two segments" legible instead of feeling like two unrelated businesses sharing a logo.

- **Film/Commercial line (legacy — verdict only, not a deep redesign):**
  Keep **"Location Department as a Service."** It's earned, accurate, and still the correct line for the ICP it targets — the fact that this segment is deprioritized for sales effort doesn't mean the line is broken; it's fine as-is. No further investment warranted here beyond leaving it alone.

- **Event line (core deliverable — new positioning needed):**
  Retire "Location Department as a Service" for this segment entirely; do not adapt/water it down. Replace with language built from what already sells and what already differentiates:

  **Recommended positioning line:**
  **"Satu Vendor, Semua Kebutuhan Event."** (One Vendor, Every Event Need.)
  Supporting line (EN, for bilingual/deck use): **"Your embedded event supplier — tenda, HT, equipment, and crew, in one call."**

  Why this over the current field workaround ("Kami merupakan vendor..."):
  - It keeps the plain, concrete "vendor" self-identification the ICP already understands (no jargon tax) — the audience for tenda/HT rental is not looking for cleverness, they're looking for competence and coverage.
  - But it upgrades from generic-vendor to **one-stop / embedded-supplier framing**, which is Durent's actual moat (context §9) and directly answers the ICP's real pain point: vendor fatigue from juggling multiple disposable suppliers per event (context §8). "One vendor, every need" is the antidote to that fatigue, stated in the buyer's own terms.
  - It doesn't claim anything Durent can't deliver today — tenda, HT, and equipment rental are confirmed as the top-selling lines to this ICP (context §5).

### 3.2 Tone of voice

- **Event segment:** Bahasa Indonesia-first, plain and transactional, price-and-package forward (mirrors how the proposal deck already works — package tables, "Contact Us for Detail" CTAs). Keep it. Don't over-polish into corporate-speak; this buyer wants a fast, clear quote and evidence of reliability, not a pitch deck. Add ONE layer the current deck is missing: a short trust/differentiation statement before the price tables (e.g., a line about being an embedded, repeat-relationship supplier, not a one-off vendor) — right now the deck jumps straight to commodity pricing with nothing to justify choosing Durent over any other tenda rental, which reinforces exactly the vendor-fatigue dynamic the company is trying to escape.
- **Film/Commercial segment:** Keep the more polished, English-forward, industry-vocabulary tone implied by "Location Department as a Service" and "AI Scout" — this ICP responds to precision and insider fluency.
- **Shared, parent-level:** Confident, direct, no fluff — reliability and follow-through as the brand's actual proof points (15 months operating, ROI 5.4x, repeat rate 29%, vendors paid on time — context §10/§9). These numbers belong in event-segment sales conversations too, reframed simply as "we've done this before and we pay people on time" — that's a meaningful trust signal against fly-by-night tenda vendors.

### 3.3 Visual direction

- No redesign needed. Carry the existing dark/moody, gold-accented, glassmorphism-card visual system from the proposal deck across all event-segment materials going forward — it already works and is segment-agnostic.
- Reuse the same wordmark lockup (dark-on-light for web/formal docs, white-on-dark for decks/social) across both segments unchanged.
- One gap: there's no evidence of a distinct visual sub-mark or color tag differentiating "Durent Support | Film" from "Durent Support | Event" in materials seen. Recommend a light-touch device (a one-word suffix tag under the wordmark — "Film & Commercial" / "Event" — same treatment as "Support" is used today) rather than any deeper visual fork. Keeps the parent brand singular while letting each segment's materials self-identify at a glance.

## 4. Explicit verdict

**The current brand position does not match where the company is strategically going.**

"Location Department as a Service" is a well-built, accurate identity for the segment the company is *stepping back from* (film/commercial), and it is absent — by the company's own field practice — from the segment it is *betting on* (event). The parent brand (name, mark, visual system) is strong, flexible, and doesn't need to change. What's missing is a deliberate, differentiated positioning line for the event segment that upgrades the ad-hoc "we are a vendor" language already in market into something that carries Durent's actual differentiation (embedded supplier, one-stop integration, reliability) — because right now, in the segment that matters most, Durent's brand says nothing that a generic tenda-rental competitor couldn't also say.

**Priority action:** adopt the event-segment line above (or a close variant), fold "embedded supplier" into the parent-brand frame so both segments read as one coherent company, and leave the film-segment line untouched.
