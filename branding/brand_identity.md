## 1. What the existing brand assets actually say

### 1.1 The name and mark
"DuRent" = **durian + rent** — a local wordplay pun. The logo renders the "D" as a spiky durian silhouette (visible husk texture on the ascender), set in a bold, rounded, geometric sans-serif, dark near-black on transparent, with "Support" in a smaller matching weight underneath. It's playful, distinctly Indonesian/local-market (durian is a culturally loaded, affectionate reference — not neutral like a generic Latin-root name), and confident without being cute.

Critically: **the pun is about the company's function (rental) and locale (durian = Southeast Asian), not about film or locations specifically.** Nothing in the mark itself is film-industry-coded. It's also, per the domain check below (§2.2), a genuinely distinctive name in a category full of generic descriptive names ("Raja Alat Event," "Seventent," "Akusewa") — worth protecting and standardizing, not diluting across inconsistent copy.

### 1.2 The stated positioning (per `durent-context.md` §3)
> "Location Department as a Service" — website live, 20+ locations onboarded, "AI Scout" for location scouting.

This is industry-insider language. "Location department" is a specific film/TV production-department term (the crew function that scouts, secures, and manages shoot locations). It's a sharp, credible line **to a producer or production manager** — it signals "we are the outsourced version of a function you already know you need." It communicates nothing to someone who has never worked on a film set.

### 1.3 What the company's actual event sales material says (Proposal Event support.pdf)
This is the more revealing artifact. It's a live, in-market proposal deck used with an actual event prospect (a university wisuda/graduation ceremony). Its self-description, verbatim:

> "Kami merupakan vendor yang menyediakan keperluan event seperti: Penyewaan barang-barang event (Tenda, Meja, Kipas, dsb.), Peralatan komunikasi (HT dan Solidcom), Penyediaan Konsumsi (Nasi box dan Prasmanan). Semua layanan terintegrasi menjadi satu di ekosistem durent support."
> ("We are a vendor providing event necessities... All services are integrated into one ecosystem, durent support.")

The phrase **"Location Department as a Service" does not appear anywhere in this document.** When Durent actually had to sell to an event buyer, the team didn't stretch the film positioning to fit — they dropped it and wrote plain, generic "vendor" copy instead. That's not a hypothetical brand-fit problem; it's evidence the fit problem is already live and the team has already, informally, worked around it. The workaround (generic "vendor," "terintegrasi menjadi satu ekosistem") is functional but — per the category scan in §2.3 below — it's also exactly the same claim every competitor already makes. It doesn't yet carry Durent's *actual*, harder-to-copy differentiation.

### 1.4 Visual system (from the proposal deck — the only in-market event-facing visual asset available)
- Dark, near-black/navy backdrops; moody stadium-floodlight and city-skyline photography, desaturated.
- Bold, rounded, high-weight sans-serif headlines (same type family as the wordmark) in white.
- A restrained gold/champagne accent line — the one "premium" signal in an otherwise monochrome palette.
- Glassmorphism pill-shaped cards for package/price tables — clean, modern, price-forward.
- Product-cutout photography (tents, chairs, AC units, sound equipment) over the mood backgrounds.

This visual system is **not film-specific.** Stadium floodlights and dark cinematic staging read equally well as "concert/event production" or "film set" — it's an atmosphere of scale and professionalism, not a location-scouting visual. **This is good news: the visual system does not need to be rebuilt or split by segment.** It already generalizes.

## 2. Brand coherence assessment

### 2.1 Does "Location Department as a Service" work for both segments?

**No — and the company's own sales material already proves it doesn't.**

| | Film/Commercial | Event |
|---|---|---|
| Positioning fit | Strong — "location department" is exact insider vocabulary for this ICP | Meaningless — bazaar/campus/expo organizers don't have or think in terms of a "location department" |
| What ICP actually wants (context §5) | Locations, crew, equipment, catering as a production support function | Tenda, HT, equipment rental from a full-service vendor — commodity-adjacent, price- and reliability-driven |
| Evidence of mismatch | — | Company's own proposal deck abandoned the tagline entirely in favor of generic "vendor" language |
| Strategic weight now (context §4) | Deprioritized — sales stalled here | **Priority** — the segment being actively grown |

The mark and visual system flex fine across both. **The positioning line does not.** Forcing "Location Department as a Service" onto the event segment would be actively counterproductive: it's off-vocabulary for the buyer, undersells the tenda/HT/equipment offer that's actually converting, and doesn't address the one real objection this ICP has (vendor fatigue, context §8).

### 2.2 Brand-hygiene flag: duplicate/stale domain (competitor-analyst finding)

Public search surfaces **durent-support.my.id** — a *different* domain from the canonical `durentsupport.com` — describing the company as offering "complete production solutions — equipment rentals, professional crew support, and trusted logistics." That copy is a third, distinct positioning voice, separate from both "Location Department as a Service" (site, per context doc) and "vendor... terintegrasi menjadi satu ekosistem" (event proposal deck).

This means there are currently **three different public/semi-public descriptions of what Durent Support is**, none matching the others. That's a real, fixable brand-hygiene problem independent of the film/event strategy question:
- **Verify which domain is canonical** — is `.my.id` an abandoned earlier build, a parallel site someone still maintains, or a squat/duplicate? Unclear from here; needs a direct owner check.
- If stale: **redirect or kill it** — a duplicate domain with inconsistent copy actively confuses prospects who search the company name and undermines SEO (search engines and buyers both penalize/distrust inconsistent identity across domains).
- If still in active use for some purpose: **align its copy** to whichever positioning house style is adopted (see §3), rather than leaving a third freelance description in circulation.

Recommend this go on the owner's fundamentals checklist, not just the brand-strategy backlog — it's a quick fix with outsized downside if left alone (a prospect who finds the wrong domain first forms a first impression from copy nobody is intentionally maintaining).

### 2.3 Category positioning scan: "one-stop" is not a differentiator (competitor-analyst finding)

Competitor-analyst's scan of the event-rental category (Eventara, Raja Alat Event, Akusewa, Seventent, and others) found that **"one-stop event solution" / "semua kebutuhan dalam satu tempat" is already the category's standard marketing copy** — not a Durent-specific claim. Notably, **Raja Alat Event** overlaps Durent's own name/positioning territory directly, which sharpens the need for language that's actually distinctive rather than descriptively generic.

The sharper finding: **no incumbent actually bundles cross-category coordination.** Equipment rental, HT/comms, crew/manpower, catering, and transport are, in this market, separate industries run by separate vendors that don't naturally cross-sell each other. Everyone *claims* "one-stop," but "one-stop" in practice usually just means one company with a wide catalog in one category (e.g., a tenda vendor who also stocks chairs and AC units) — not a company that coordinates genuinely different trades under one operational plan.

That's the real opening, and it changes the recommended positioning below (§3.1): the event-segment brand promise should be **coordination across categories**, not **breadth of rental inventory**. "One-stop" as a phrase is now a liability, not an asset — it signals "same as everyone else" to a buyer who has already heard it from five other vendors.

One more correction this scan forces: **"embedded supplier" should not be pitched as a status Durent already uniquely holds.** Per this finding, incumbents in the category already function as embedded/repeat suppliers to the organizers they've served before — that's the market's normal relationship model, not something Durent alone offers. Claiming it as a differentiator would overstate the company's actual position and could read as hollow to a buyer who already has an embedded relationship with someone else. It's more honest and more useful framed as **an aspiration/relationship promise Durent makes to a specific client going forward** ("we want to become this for you"), not a claimed market position.

## 3. Recommendations

### 3.1 Brand architecture: one parent, two segment lines

Keep **Durent Support** as the single parent/holding brand — the name, mark, and visual system are sound and shouldn't change. Under it, run two distinct positioning lines, not one stretched to cover both:

- **Parent-level frame (holding, investor-facing, footer/about-us level):**
  **"Durent Support — coordinated production & event operations in Jabodetabek."**
  This replaces an earlier draft of this line that leaned on "embedded supplier" as a headline claim — per §2.3, that's the market's existing norm, not Durent's edge, and overclaiming it is a real risk now that it's been flagged. "Coordinated operations" is the throughline that's actually true of both segments (a film location department coordinates locations/crew/equipment/catering; an event operation coordinates tenda/HT/crew/catering/transport) and it's defensible rather than aspirational.

- **Film/Commercial line (legacy — verdict only, not a deep redesign):**
  Keep **"Location Department as a Service."** It's earned, accurate, and still the correct line for the ICP it targets — the fact that this segment is deprioritized for sales effort doesn't mean the line is broken; it's fine as-is. No further investment warranted here beyond leaving it alone.

- **Event line (core deliverable — new positioning needed):**
  Retire "Location Department as a Service" for this segment entirely — and, per §2.3, do **not** replace it with another "one-stop vendor" claim; that phrase is now category wallpaper, used near-verbatim by Eventara, Raja Alat Event, Akusewa, and Seventent. The differentiated ground is coordination across categories that don't normally cross-sell, not inventory breadth.

  **Recommended positioning line:**
  **"Satu Operasi, Bukan Lima Vendor."** (One Operation, Not Five Vendors.)
  Supporting line (EN, for bilingual/deck use): **"We run your event's equipment, comms, crew, catering, and transport as one coordinated operation — not five separate vendors you have to manage yourself."**

  Why this over both the current field workaround *and* the earlier "one vendor, every need" draft:
  - It names the buyer's actual pain (context §8: vendor fatigue, chasing quotes from disconnected suppliers) and frames Durent as the fix for *coordination*, not just as a bigger catalog — which is the one thing the category scan confirms nobody else is actually claiming.
  - It's provable, not aspirational: tenda, HT, equipment, crew, and catering are all confirmed live service lines (context §2, §5) — the claim is "we coordinate what we already do," not a new promise.
  - It stays honest about "embedded supplier" (§2.3): that language now belongs in the relationship-building conversation with a specific repeat client ("we want to be your standing operations partner"), not in the top-line brand claim to a first-time prospect.

### 3.2 Tone of voice

- **Event segment:** Bahasa Indonesia-first, plain and transactional, price-and-package forward (mirrors how the proposal deck already works — package tables, "Contact Us for Detail" CTAs). Keep that mechanic. Don't over-polish into corporate-speak; this buyer wants a fast, clear quote and evidence of reliability, not a pitch deck. Add ONE layer the current deck is missing: a short coordination/trust statement before the price tables — right now the deck jumps straight to commodity pricing with nothing to justify choosing Durent over any other tenda rental, which reinforces exactly the vendor-fatigue dynamic the company is trying to escape. A single line like "Satu tim, satu koordinasi, untuk semua kebutuhan fisik acara Anda" ahead of the package tables would do this without slowing down the deck's fast, price-led rhythm.
- **Film/Commercial segment:** Keep the more polished, English-forward, industry-vocabulary tone implied by "Location Department as a Service" and "AI Scout" — this ICP responds to precision and insider fluency.
- **Shared, parent-level:** Confident, direct, no fluff — reliability and follow-through as the brand's actual proof points (15 months operating, ROI 5.4x, repeat rate 29%, vendors paid on time — context §9/§10). These numbers belong in event-segment sales conversations too, reframed simply as "we've done this before and we pay people on time" — a meaningful trust signal against fly-by-night tenda vendors, and one that supports the coordination claim without relying on the now-risky "embedded supplier" language.

### 3.3 Visual direction

- No redesign needed. Carry the existing dark/moody, gold-accented, glassmorphism-card visual system from the proposal deck across all event-segment materials going forward — it already works and is segment-agnostic.
- Reuse the same wordmark lockup (dark-on-light for web/formal docs, white-on-dark for decks/social) across both segments unchanged.
- One gap: there's no evidence of a distinct visual sub-mark or color tag differentiating "Durent Support | Film" from "Durent Support | Event" in materials seen. Recommend a light-touch device (a one-word suffix tag under the wordmark — "Film & Commercial" / "Event" — same treatment as "Support" is used today) rather than any deeper visual fork. Keeps the parent brand singular while letting each segment's materials self-identify at a glance.

### 3.4 Site gap analysis (requested by strat)

Could not verify durentsupport.com directly — network egress blocked in this environment, and it's not indexed by search either. Based on `durent-context.md`'s description of the site (positioning = "Location Department as a Service," AI Scout for location scouting, 20+ locations onboarded), the event proposal deck's completely disconnected brand voice, and now the third, further-inconsistent copy found on `durent-support.my.id` (§2.2), the reasonable inference is: **the canonical site as described speaks only to film/commercial buyers today; there is no visible, coordinated event-segment presence across any of the company's public-facing surfaces.** This is inferred, not confirmed — flagging as **pending owner/site-content confirmation**. Given three inconsistent descriptions are now on record, recommend this becomes a short, concrete task (owner or whoever holds site access does a pass across both domains) rather than staying an open question in strategy docs.

## 4. Explicit verdict

**The current brand position does not match where the company is strategically going — and the gap is wider than a single stale tagline.**

"Location Department as a Service" is a well-built, accurate identity for the segment the company is *stepping back from* (film/commercial), and it is absent — by the company's own field practice — from the segment it is *betting on* (event). Worse, in the event segment's absence of a real positioning, the company has accumulated **three different unmanaged descriptions of itself** (site tagline, event proposal copy, and a separate `.my.id` domain's copy) — none of which say the one thing that's actually true and actually differentiated: that Durent coordinates categories (equipment, comms, crew, catering, transport) that the rest of the category sells separately.

The parent brand (name, mark, visual system) is strong, flexible, and doesn't need to change. What's missing is: (1) a deliberate, differentiated positioning line for the event segment built on coordination rather than "one-stop" catalog breadth — a claim the whole category already makes — and (2) basic brand hygiene to collapse three inconsistent public descriptions into one.

**Priority actions:**
1. Adopt the coordination-based event-segment line (§3.1) in place of both the unused film tagline and the ad-hoc "vendor" copy currently in market.
2. Resolve the domain duplication (§2.2) — confirm canonical domain, kill or align the other.
3. Retire "embedded supplier" as a headline brand claim; keep it as a relationship promise made to specific clients, not a market-positioning statement.
4. Leave the film-segment line and the visual system untouched — neither is the problem.
