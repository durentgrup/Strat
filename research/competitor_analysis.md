## 1. Headline findings

1. **The "one-stop event equipment" position Durent is pitching is already occupied — heavily.**
   At least seven established Jabodetabek players market almost the identical line ("one stop
   event solution", "sewa alat event terlengkap"), several of them PTs with 10–16 years of
   operating history and their own logistics fleets. "One pintu for everything" is not a
   differentiator in this market; it is the category's standard marketing copy.

2. **But nobody bundles what Durent actually bundles.** The incumbents bundle *equipment*
   (tenda, sound, panggung, LED, genset, AC, booth). They do **not** sell HT, crew catering,
   manpower, or transport. Those sit with three separate industries — radio-comms rental shops,
   caterers, and SPG/usher agencies. **The real white space is cross-category coordination, not
   equipment breadth.** This is the single most important finding in the document and it should
   reshape how Durent describes itself.

3. **The supplier/competitor dual role is real and it is the central strategic problem.**
   If Durent brokers rather than owns (see §7.1 — this is an open question, not an assertion),
   then Eventara, Akusewa, Over Organizer, Raja Alat Event and Seventent are simultaneously
   Durent's supply base *and* its rivals for the same organizer. They sell direct, at their own
   cost, with owned inventory, from page one of Google.

4. **Durent's average job is smaller than a single specialist vendor's minimum order.**
   Durent's average job is Rp 1.7 juta (context doc §10). One Jakarta tent specialist publishes a
   **minimum order of Rp 2.5 juta** for in-city Jakarta and **Rp 5 juta** outside it [snippet].
   Durent is currently transacting below the floor at which the specialists will even pick up the
   phone. The Rp 50 juta/event ambition is roughly a **30× jump** from today's average ticket —
   that is the gap to close, and it is a gap in *deal size*, not in service breadth.

5. **PT registration is a gate, not a moat.** Every serious competitor found is already a PT
   (PT Event Nusantara Indonesia, PT Raja Alat Event Indonesia, PT Sentra Vendor Tenda,
   PT Askara Connecting Indonesia). PT + NIB + NPWP + SIUP + akta is the *entry requirement* for
   LPSE government tender, not an edge over rivals. It unlocks a channel Durent cannot currently
   access — which is valuable — but it does not differentiate Durent against anyone already in
   that channel.

6. **Peerspace and Giggster are not competitors. They never were.** Neither operates in
   Indonesia (§8.1). They are useful *model references*. The context doc treats them as
   "kompetitor" and that framing should be corrected — it is currently inflating the sense of
   white space.

7. **IFFa is not what the context doc says it is, and the "dormant" read is directionally right
   but strategically misleading.** IFFa is not a competitor company; it is a state-backed
   *location-permitting portal* built by PFN (a BUMN) with Telkom, whose published 2024 roadmap
   was to add **talent, equipment, and crew** — i.e. to become Durent's business, with government
   backing (§8.2). Dormant today, but this is a latent threat, not empty white space.

## 2. Landscape map — event support, Jabodetabek

The market splits into six layers. Durent is currently trying to stand across four of them at once.

| Layer | What they sell | Relationship to Durent |
|---|---|---|
| **A. One-stop equipment houses** | Tenda, sound, panggung, LED, genset, AC, dekorasi — bundled, own fleet | **Direct competitor AND likely supplier** |
| **B. Tent specialists** | Tenda roder/sarnafil/kerucut/bazar only, deep inventory | Supplier; competitor on tent-only jobs |
| **C. HT / radio-comms rental** | Handy-talkie, repeater, POC/GSM radio | Supplier; competitor on HT-only jobs |
| **D. Exhibition/booth contractors** | Custom booth fabrication at ICE BSD / JIExpo / JCC | **Not** a competitor — different capability (owns workshop). Potential partner/channel |
| **E. Manpower agencies** | SPG, usher, event crew, LO, team leader | Supplier for Durent's crew line; competitor on crew-only jobs |
| **F. Marketplaces / directories** | Listings, Tokopedia/Shopee rental storefronts | Channel threat — commoditises price discovery |

**The structural insight:** layers A–C–E are *different industries* that do not cross-sell to each
other. An organizer needing tenda + HT + crew today runs three separate procurement threads.
That friction is Durent's actual product. Nothing in the research contradicts this, and it is the
one claim in Durent's pitch that survives competitive scrutiny intact.

## 5. How competitors price — rate card vs quote-per-project *(strat Q1)*

The market is **split, and the split is informative**:

**Publish a rate card** — Over Organizer, Sewa Tenda Roder, Tenda Intan Ali, Mentari Sewa Tenda,
Fahira Tenda, Global Pesta, Pinjam Kursi, Akusewa (package prices). Note that INPRO has a
publicly-hosted PDF price list. These are, notably, the **category specialists and the
Jabodetabek-focused players**.

**Quote-per-project only** — Eventara, Sakata, DETA, most of the "one-stop" and
exhibition-contractor tier. Their CTA is a free WhatsApp consultation.

**The pattern:** the *broader* the offering, the *less likely* it is priced publicly. Complexity
is the excuse for opacity.

**Three implications for Durent:**

1. **Durent's internal rate card / price book (context §8) is correct and is also market-normal.**
   It is not just an internal fix for vendor fatigue — publishing it would put Durent in the
   credible-transparent camp alongside Over Organizer rather than the opaque camp.
2. **But transparency is dangerous for a broker.** If Durent publishes prices on commodity items
   whose upstream rate is *also* published (tenda at Rp 25–60k/m², kursi at Rp 12.5k/unit), the
   markup is arithmetic any buyer can do. See §7.
3. **The escape is to price the bundle, not the unit.** Package/coordination pricing (Akusewa's
   model) hides the per-unit markup legitimately, because the buyer is genuinely purchasing
   something the unit price does not describe.

**All-in pricing is the norm buyers expect.** Over Organizer includes Jabodetabek transport in
listed prices; Akusewa includes free Jabodetabek delivery plus install/dismantle; Seventent
includes free install [snippet]. **Any Durent quote that adds transport or install as a line item
will read as more expensive than it is.** Quote all-in.

## 6. How organizers find and choose vendors *(strat Q2)*

Synthesised from Indonesian-language event-planning and vendor-selection guidance found in
research [snippet]:

**How they search:**
- Google, on very specific long-tail Indonesian phrases: `sewa tenda bazar`, `sewa HT Jakarta`,
  `sewa alat event murah`, and **location-suffixed** variants (`sewa tenda jualan Depok`,
  `sewa tenda acara Tangerang`). Competitors invest heavily here — Seventent and Zein have
  dedicated pages per city and per event-type.
- **Social media / Instagram** — guidance explicitly tells committees to "berburu lewat media
  sosial" and to review vendors' past-event documentation.
- **Marketplaces** (Tokopedia/Shopee) for commodity items.
- **Referral** — consistent with Durent's own experience (all 11 Q1 clients came from warm
  referral).

**How they decide — the stated selection criteria:**
1. **Documentation of past events** and customer reviews as the proxy for quality
2. **Whether the quoted price already includes installation, dismantle, transport, and tax** —
   buyers are explicitly warned to "hindari biaya tersembunyi"
3. **Whether the vendor helps them size the job** — "vendor profesional biasanya akan membantu
   Anda menghitung ukuran tenda berdasarkan jumlah undangan." Advisory capability is a stated
   purchase criterion, not a nicety.
4. Visiting the vendor / asking for a track record directly

**Three things this tells Durent:**

- **Durent is likely invisible in the channel where these buyers actually look.** The competitors
  above own the Indonesian long-tail search terms. Public search for Durent surfaced
  `durent-support.my.id`, not `durentsupport.com` — and described it as *"complete production
  solutions—equipment rentals, professional crew support, and trusted logistics for films,
  events, or creative projects"*, **not** "Location Department as a Service." **[Flag for
  brand-specialist and the owner: the stated positioning does not match what is publicly
  indexed, and there may be a domain/SEO problem.]**
- **Criterion 3 is Durent's opening.** "We help you size the job" is exactly what an organizer
  without a vendor network needs, and it is the criterion the commodity vendors satisfy least
  well. It is also unpaid consulting unless it is structured — which argues for making
  *coordination* the billable product.
- **Criterion 2 argues for all-in quoting**, per §5.

## 7. So what for Durent

### 7.1 The spine: supplier and competitor are the same companies

**Open question for the owner — do not let this be assumed either way:** *Does Durent own its
tenda and HT inventory, or does it sub-rent from third-party vendors?* Nothing in
`durent-context.md` states this. The strongest available signal is §8's "vendor fatigue" —
Durent repeatedly requests quotes from vendors, which reads as brokered. §9's moat list
(written vendor partnership agreements, pay-vendors-on-time track record) points the same way.
**But it is inference, not fact, and the owner should answer it before this becomes strategy.**

**If Durent is brokering, the following is true:**

- Eventara, Akusewa, Over Organizer, Raja Alat Event, Seventent and the tent houses are
  **simultaneously Durent's supply base and its rivals for the same organizer.** They reach the
  same buyer directly, at their own cost, with owned inventory and 10–16 years of portfolio.
- **Durent's margin is structurally exposed.** Over Organizer publishes Rp 25–60k/m² for tents
  and Rp 12.5k/unit for chairs. Durent's reported **56% gross margin** implies COGS at 44% of
  price — roughly a **2.3× markup**. On items whose upstream price is one search away, that is
  not defensible as a pure rental markup. It is only defensible if the buyer understands they
  are paying for coordination, sourcing, and single-point accountability — i.e. **if the product
  is reframed.** *(Caveat: the 56% is blended across five service lines; the equipment-only
  margin is unknown. Worth the owner decomposing it.)*
- **Bypass risk is real and it is not the same risk as on the film side.** The context doc
  describes an "anti-bypass value architecture" for *location partners*. There is no equivalent
  protection when the supplier is a nationally-marketed PT with its own inbound funnel. An
  organizer who sees "Eventara" on the delivered tents can call Eventara next year.

### 7.2 Can Durent defend margin as a broker, or does it need to own inventory? *(strat addition 1)*

**My read: split the answer by asset class. Own HT. Broker tents. Never own booths.**

**Own HT — the case is strong:**
- Rental rates are high relative to asset cost. Baofeng-class units rent at **Rp 25.000/day**
  [snippet] against a low purchase price; a fleet of 50 is modest capex, and 50 units at 20
  event-days a year at Rp 25k is meaningful recurring gross profit against a one-time outlay.
- HT is needed at **essentially every event** — bazaar, campus, expo alike. Highest utilisation
  of anything Durent touches.
- It is the item organizers are **least likely to shop around on** (small ticket, high urgency,
  low differentiation) — so brokered markup is least discoverable, and owned margin is highest.
- Logistics are trivial: units fit in a car, no truck, no storage yard, no install crew.
- Owning it converts Durent from reseller to **the vendor of record on the one item every
  organizer needs**, creating a legitimate reason to be in the room for everything else.
- *(Requires: capital availability, which is constrained given the Rp 100 juta cash target.
  Model the fleet size against that constraint before committing.)*

**Broker tents — owning is wrong:**
- Tents are bulky, need trucks, storage, install crews, washing and repair. It is a logistics
  business with high fixed cost, and the incumbents already have fleets and 10–16 years of
  amortisation. Durent would be entering as the highest-cost operator.
- Minimum-order economics (Rp 2.5–5 juta) show the specialists themselves cannot serve small jobs
  profitably — that is a structural cost problem Durent would inherit.

**Can broker margin be defended at all?** Yes, but **only if the billable unit changes.**
The rate card + partnership agreements defend *reliability and speed*, not *price*. Suppliers
are abundant and fragmented in this market, so supply-side agreements do not create pricing
power. What Durent can defensibly charge for is:
1. **Coordination across categories nobody else bundles** (tenda + HT + crew + catering +
   transport in one contract, one invoice, one accountable party)
2. **Advisory/sizing** — a stated buyer criterion (§6) that commodity vendors serve badly
3. **Single-point accountability** — the thing an inexperienced committee is actually buying

If Durent keeps selling *"tenda, and also HT, and also crew"* as line items, the margin will be
competed away. If it sells *"we run your event's physical operations"*, it will not.

### 7.3 ICE BSD / JIExpo — barrier or entry path? *(strat addition 2)*

**Finding: the gate is at the show-organizer level, not the venue level — and that is good news
for Durent.**

- **I could not find a published official-vendor or `daftar rekanan` list for either JIExpo
  Kemayoran or ICE BSD. [UNVERIFIED — searched specifically for both; no official list surfaced.
  Should be confirmed directly with venue management, and this is a five-minute phone call.]**
- What the evidence does show: **individual show organizers appoint official contractors
  per-exhibition**, and multiple independent contractors (DETA, Total Expo, Sewa Asia) operate
  across JIExpo, ICE BSD, JCC and PIK NICE without any single venue-level monopoly appearing.
- **Therefore: entry path, not barrier — provided Durent enters at the organizer level.** Durent's
  named warm contacts at APKASI Otonomi Expo 2026 (Ervinda, Yayuk, Ray, Novia, Rina, Frema) are
  **exactly the right altitude.** That is the decision-making layer.

**Actionable before 27–29 August (five days from today):**
1. **Do not pitch booth contracting.** Layer D requires owned fabrication and rigging compliance;
   Durent cannot deliver it and will lose credibility trying.
2. **Pitch the non-booth support layer** where no incumbent is integrated: HT for the organizing
   committee, crew/LO, transport, crew catering, and outdoor/overflow tenting. These are the gaps
   *between* the appointed contractors, and they are the organizer's own headaches rather than
   the exhibitors'.
3. **Ask one question at APKASI that is worth more than the pitch:** *who appoints official
   vendors for this show, and when does that appointment happen for next year?* That answer is
   the entry key to a recurring expo, and Durent has the relationships to ask it.
4. **PT registration matters here specifically** — expo organizers and government-adjacent bodies
   like APKASI will require a badan usaha for formal appointment. Treat completing the PT as
   gating for this channel, and see §7.4.

### 7.4 Is the claimed moat actually differentiating? *(strat Q3)*

Honest assessment of `durent-context.md` §9, item by item:

| Claimed moat | Verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **PT registration (for tender access)** | **Gate, not moat** | Table stakes. LPSE registration requires badan usaha + NIB + akta + NPWP + SIUP [snippet]. Every serious competitor found is already a PT. It opens a channel; it differentiates against nobody in it. **Still worth completing — but sell it as capability, never as advantage.** |
| **Written vendor partnership agreements** | **Real but weak as a moat** | Genuinely improves reliability and quote turnaround. But supply is abundant and fragmented, so locking in suppliers creates no scarcity and therefore no pricing power. It is an *operational* asset, not a competitive one. |
| **Embedded supplier status with organizers** | **The genuinely valuable one — but not yet owned** | This is the right thing to want. It is also exactly what Eventara (10+ yrs), Zein (15+ yrs) and Raja Alat Event (since 2010) already have with their own client bases. Durent is claiming as a moat something incumbents have and it does not yet. **Reframe as the goal, not the current position.** |
| **Pay vendors on time** | **True differentiator — but on the wrong side of the market** | Given the "vendor fatigue" problem in the market, being a reliably-paying counterparty is real. But it wins *supplier* preference, not *customer* preference. It lowers Durent's cost of supply and improves responsiveness; it does not win a single organizer deal. Use it in supplier negotiation, not in the client pitch. |

**Net:** the current moat list is a supply-side list being used as a demand-side pitch. The only

### 7.5 Gaps Durent can exploit before December 2026 *(strat Q4)*

Ordered by how quickly they can produce cash against the Rp 100 juta target.

**1. Cross-category bundling — the only structural white space (highest value)**
No competitor found sells tenda + HT + crew + catering + transport as one contract. Layers A, C
and E are separate industries with no cross-selling. Durent already sells across all of them.
**This should become the entire positioning**, replacing "one-stop equipment" (occupied) and
"Location Department as a Service" (film-era, and not what the public site says anyway).

**2. Raise deal size, not deal count (the fastest path to Rp 100 juta)**
Durent's Rp 1.7 juta average job is below a specialist's Rp 2.5 juta minimum order. The Expo UMKM
benchmark says a real bazaar tent job bills ~Rp 18 juta. **Durent does not have a volume problem
so much as a ticket-size problem.** Three Rp 18 juta-class events beat thirty Rp 1.7 juta jobs on
every dimension — revenue, margin, and effort. The existing pipeline already points here (the
50-tent/13-day tender, PT Furindo Artha Mas).

**3. Campus/PKKMB — genuinely under-served (best fit to the stated ICP)**
Incumbent marketing targets corporate, wedding, exhibition and UMKM bazaar. Zein mentions
"sekolah" and Eventara mentions "school market days," but **no competitor found is positioned for
campus committees.** Campus organizers are the "no vendor network" ICP almost by definition:
budget-constrained, inexperienced, needing hand-holding, and **rotating annually — so there is a
fresh un-networked buyer every single year.**
*Double-edged, and the owner should hear both sides:* annual rotation destroys repeat-client LTV
(which matters given Durent's 29% repeat rate), so the asset to build is a relationship with the
**institution** (BEM, kemahasiswaan, rektorat) rather than with the individual committee.
Also note the repo has a `campus-event-researcher` skill — that capability already exists in-house.

**4. Own the HT fleet (per §7.2)** — small capex, highest utilisation, least price-discoverable,
converts Durent from reseller to vendor-of-record.

**5. Publish a rate card — but for bundles, not units (per §5)**
Matches the credible-transparent market norm, fixes vendor fatigue, and speeds sales — while
avoiding exposing per-unit markup on commodities. Quote all-in (transport + install + dismantle
+ tax included), because that is what buyers are told to demand.

**6. Outbound + nurture is a channel the incumbents do not run**
Every competitor found is SEO/inbound-dependent. Durent's 100+ cold emails → ~12 replies, with
the "event still far off" pattern, is a channel the incumbents are not contesting. Combined with
the campus segment's predictable annual calendar, a long nurture cadence built around known event
dates is a genuine unfair advantage. **This is Durent's cheapest edge and it needs no capital.**

### 8.2 Correction: what IFFa actually is — and why "dormant" understates the risk

`durent-context.md` §3 describes IFFa as "kompetitor dari sisi pemerintah… praktis dormant →
white space masih kosong."

**What IFFa actually is [snippet]:**
- **Indonesia Film Facilitation** — a digital *portal*, not a company. Built by **PT Produksi
  Film Negara (PFN)**, a state-owned enterprise, with **Kemenparekraf**, and developed with
  **Telkom Indonesia via its subsidiary Nuon Digital**. Live at `iffa.id`, launched ~November 2023.
- **Function:** streamlines **shooting-location permitting** — location search, location costs,
  permit procedures — with a stated commitment that permitting authorities and location managers
  respond within **seven business days**.
- **Its published 2024 roadmap was to expand into cast/talent, equipment, and film crew** — i.e.
  precisely Durent's five service lines.

**On the "dormant" claim:** substantive coverage clusters in **November 2023** at launch. I found
no evidence of the promised 2024 service expansion actually shipping, and no significant IFFa
activity reporting in 2025–2026. Separately, PFN remains active and government-backed — as of
July 2025 Kemenekraf was pushing PFN toward becoming a "State Content Center," and an
**Indonesia Creative Content Fund (ICCF)** was under proposal as of December 2025 [snippet].
**I could not load `iffa.id` to confirm current operational status — egress blocked. [UNVERIFIED]**

**So the internal claim is directionally right but the conclusion is wrong.** "Dormant, therefore
white space is empty" understates the risk. The accurate read: **a state-backed entity with
Telkom engineering support has publicly announced an intention to build Durent's business, has
not executed, and sits inside an actively-funded government push for the film sector.** That is
not empty white space — that is a **latent, well-capitalised entrant with policy backing.**
Durent should build relationships and locations that would make it a *partner* to IFFa if IFFa
revives, rather than assuming the space stays clear. Worth a five-minute check of `iffa.id`
by anyone with normal web access.

## 9. Summary judgement

Durent's event pivot is aimed at the right market, but the **stated differentiation will not
survive contact with it.** "One-stop event solution" is the incumbents' own marketing copy, run by
PTs with 10–16 years of history, owned fleets, published price lists and ownership of the search
terms buyers use. Meanwhile Durent's claimed moat (PT, vendor agreements, pay-on-time) is a
supply-side list being pitched to the demand side.

The genuinely defensible position is available and Durent is already sitting on it: **no vendor
in this market bundles tenda + HT + crew + catering + transport, because those are four separate
industries.** That coordination — sold to organizers who have no vendor network, at ticket sizes
ten times Durent's current average, with HT owned rather than brokered — is a real business.
Selling the same components as line items is not.

The most urgent unresolved fact is **owned vs brokered inventory** (§7.6). It determines whether
the margin is defensible or arithmetic.
